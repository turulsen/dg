Pursuit

RollsSeries of Opposed Tests by pursuer and quarry to reach 1–3 successes/wins: successes cancel each other out. If both fail, lowest failure wins; critical worth two successes, fumble worth two failures.
SkillsAthletics, Drive, Pilot, Heavy Machinery, Swim, Ride
AdvantagesCoordination, air support, and being faster/more maneuverable add +20%/+40%
Seeking an EdgeInstead of rolling to catch or get away, make an opposed test with another skill (e.g. Alertness, Navigate, Stealth, Survival, Tactics). If successful, neither side wins, but gain +20% to the next roll (critical: +40%).
CombatEach test is 1 turn. Pursuer attacks, quarry gets a win; vehicle passengers attack separately.

Responding to SAN Loss

Adaptation to ViolenceTake 3 violence SAN losses without suffering insanity; lose 1D6 CHA and the same amount from each Bond. Automatically succeed at SAN tests for Violence.
Adaptation to HelplessnessTake 3 helplessness SAN losses without suffering insanity; lose 1D6 CHA and the same amount from each Bond. Automatically succeed at SAN tests for Helplessness.
Projecting TraumaReduce WP, a Bond, and the SAN loss by 1D4.
Temporary InsanityLose 5+ SAN at once (Optional: if from an unnatural source, gain 1D6 to an Unnatural Skill).
New DisorderSAN reaches the Breaking Point.
Projection (repress insanity)Reduce WP and a Bond by 1D4 to make a SAN test; success prevents temp. insanity or disorder.
Destroying the UnnaturalDestroying an unnatural creature or object may restore SAN equal to the lowest amount it could inflict, never restoring above maximum SAN. Every Agent involved gains this SAN.

Temporary Insanity (Handler chooses one): Flee (run away incoherently for turns equal to CON, or until danger is gone) · Fight (mindlessly attack anyone in sight) · Submit (curl into a helpless ball).
Mental Disorder: if facing the trigger of a disorder, make a SAN roll or the disorder takes over, as described.
Self-Medicating: Benzos add +20%, Alcohol/Cannabis add +10%; both give −20% to all skill and stat tests; both risk Addiction (which loses the bonus and keeps the penalty).

Tradecraft

The Mission & Standing Orders

As an agent, you protect the U.S. and humanity itself from unnatural threats so deadly that mere awareness of them is dangerous. It may be necessary to violate every law and principle that you once swore to uphold. You must never tell anyone about the Program, nor about the work you do on its behalf. You may die, be crippled, go insane, or be imprisoned, and your family will never be told the truth. You're told the mission is worth the sacrifice.

First Priority: Stop the incursion. Second Priority: Minimize awareness of the incursion in order to prevent the damage from spreading. Third Priority: Obscure the Program's involvement. Secure or eliminate all evidence that an unnatural event has occurred. Fourth Priority: Secure evidence, samples, documents, and advanced technology for collection. Fifth Priority: Save lives. As many as you can.

Cover Story: give first-responders, reporters, and witnesses a plausible pretext for your presence. Make sure they have no reason to investigate your team.

Cover Identity & Communications

The Program can easily provide cover identities and temporary deputization with nearly any branch of U.S. government. Its go-to cover agency is the FBI, which has the broadest jurisdiction over federal law enforcement. Always have a false identity ready to show, but rely on it only as a last resort — no false identity will stand up to sustained investigation.

False Name, False Employer: may be no better than a doctored badge or driver's license. A more extensive legend — with falsified employers, tax returns, and social-media history — takes weeks to concoct.
True Name, False Employer: intelligence officers and special operators are often employed under their own names by deniable front companies. Such a front is often a real company with many employees and years of history.

Mobile devices leave records at cell towers and internet service providers, allowing investigators to trace your movements and contacts. A dedicated SIGINT team can eavesdrop. The Program sometimes provides disposable, heavily-encrypted cellphones — untraceable movements, but eavesdropping is still possible. Phone numbers must never be programmed in, only memorized, and call histories deleted frequently. Program-issued tablets and computers are password-protected and erased after each operation. Sensitive data should never be stored on these devices.

Witnesses & Handover

Agents must decide how to handle witnesses to minimize exposure. Cooperation: occasionally a witness quickly grasps the need for secrecy and can be trusted to cooperate. Obfuscation: many witnesses can be convinced they didn't really see anything unnatural — a gas leak, a hallucinogen, a toxin in the water, or convincing them privately that reporting it will look ridiculous. Disinformation: a witness who saw too much but refuses to cooperate must be persuaded it never happened; tell your case officer, who may discredit them publicly. Sanction: if all else fails and a witness attempts to make persuasive evidence public, agents may be dispatched to silence them permanently.

Your case officer may direct you to turn physical evidence, artifacts, books, biological samples, or even living suspects over for analysis. Such a handover should be done away from public view.

Firearms: if a shooting is justifiable by your cover story, using your duty weapon may raise the fewest questions. If not, make sure you have a throwaway weapon, a way to dispose of the body without drawing attention, or a patsy to blame for the shooting.
